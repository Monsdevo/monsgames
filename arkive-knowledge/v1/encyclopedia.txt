A Short Reference to Earth, Time, and Measurement

EARTH AND THE SKY
Earth rotates around its axis about once a day. As a location rotates toward the Sun it enters daylight, and it enters night as it turns away. The seasons follow from Earth's tilted axis. During summer in a hemisphere the Sun is generally higher in the sky and daylight lasts longer. The two hemispheres have opposite seasons.

The Moon shines by reflecting sunlight. Its phases result from how much of the illuminated side we can see as it orbits Earth. A lunar eclipse occurs when the Moon passes through Earth's shadow. A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon passes between the Sun and the observer and covers part or all of the Sun.

GEOGRAPHY
Latitude measures how far north or south a location lies from the equator. Longitude measures its position east or west of the prime meridian. A map projection converts a curved surface to a plane. Every projection distorts some combination of area, shape, distance, or direction. Choose the projection according to what you want to compare.

MEASUREMENT
The International System of Units uses the metre for length, the kilogram for mass, and the second for time. Speed is distance divided by time. A kilometre per hour equals one thousand metres divided by 3,600 seconds. Mass describes how much matter an object contains in everyday usage. Weight is the gravitational force acting on it.

TIME AND CALENDARS
A civil time zone is a legal convention, so its boundaries often follow political borders. UTC is a common reference for comparing times. The Gregorian calendar uses leap years to keep the calendar aligned with the seasons. A year divisible by four is usually a leap year. Century years must also be divisible by 400.
