Bad Haunts / The concept

The room tells two different stories.

Humans search for evidence and the anchors binding each Haunt to the world. The Haunts disrupt that investigation in real time—flickering lights, sabotaging mirrors, breaking up photography and creating uncertainty.

01

Mirrors

Mirrors can reveal Haunts and help expose the objects binding them to the world. What appears in the reflection matters.

02

Cameras

Photography is an active tool. Images taken directly—or through mirrors—produce the evidence needed to seal anchors.

Devlog 001Development note

From Ship to Motel

A playable blockout proved the core loop. It also made the next decision clear: leave the earlier ship concept behind and build a map with a tighter gameplay focus.

Read updateClose update

The temporary blockout level gave the core playable logic a place to be tested. With that foundation working, development moved away from an earlier ship-map concept that was too ambitious for the project’s current scope.

The new motel is designed around the actual match: a central courtyard, multiple rooms, two levels, balconies, stairs and a swimming pool. Construction and early polish are now underway.

Online work is moving alongside the map. Steam lobby systems have been tested across two physical Windows PCs with separate accounts. HUD and menu UI are being redesigned, and music has progressed significantly.

There is still substantial work ahead in sound design, onboarding, animation, match feedback, final level art and supporting systems. Bad Haunts is not production-ready, has no announced release date, and its Steam page is expected after September 12.

About Mons Games

Small by design.
Serious about the work.

Mons Games is an independent game studio focused on systems that bring players together and give them room to create their own stories.

Its current project, Bad Haunts, combines asymmetric multiplayer design with horror, comedy and player-driven chaos. The studio is creator-led, works in the open when there is meaningful progress to share, and leaves room for future games to establish identities of their own.
