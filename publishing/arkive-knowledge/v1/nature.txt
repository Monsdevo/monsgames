Ecosystems and Natural Cycles

ECOSYSTEMS
An ecosystem includes living organisms and their physical environment. Producers such as plants and algae turn light into chemical energy through photosynthesis. Consumers obtain energy by eating organisms or their products. Decomposers break down dead material and return nutrients to the environment. Energy moves through these relationships, while materials such as carbon and nitrogen cycle.

WATER AND CLIMATE
Evaporation moves water from surfaces into the atmosphere. Cooling can cause water vapour to condense, producing clouds or fog. Precipitation returns water to the surface, where it may flow in rivers, soak into soil, or accumulate as snow. Weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular time. Climate describes patterns over a longer period.

ADAPTATION
Individuals in a population vary. When inherited traits affect survival or reproduction in a given environment, those traits can become more or less common over generations. This process is natural selection. A trait that is helpful in one environment may be costly in another.

BIODIVERSITY
Biodiversity includes the variety of genes, species, and ecosystems. Pollination, seed dispersal, and decomposition depend on relationships among organisms. Habitat loss and fragmentation can interrupt those relationships by reducing the space, food, or movement routes available to a population.
